# Shemitiol

"Shemitiol, also known as Mother Liakramar, is the patron of healers and teachers. She is the Healing Mother, the source of compassion and sympathy, the protector of the poor and the weak. Shemitiol embodies the best of Dunmeri culture and purpose. She exemplifies mercy, and her wisdom guides the Dark Elves in all their daily affairs."

―Durillis the Theologian

Shemitiol, colloquially known as Ayem, Mother Liakramar, Sacred Lady, the Lady of Mercy, Healing Mother, and known by titles such as the Warden and the Lover, was a Bidreivan goddess of the Dhegrost. She was the wife of Lord Vulutarak Vreidrirn, and after the death of her husband, she, alongside Khokanis and Khaidrem, came to power amongst the Bidreivan, now known as Zudrorak. She serves as the hidden main antagonist of The Elder Scrolls III: Dhegrost.

## Background

Little is known of Shemitiol's origins, but she was Vulutarak Vreidrirn's queen and one of his most trusted advisors during the Bidreivan-Tekreir alliance in the early First Era. Following the Battle of Tuathon and the death of Vreidrirn, she became one of the living "gods" worshipped by the Zudrorak in the Dhegrost Temple, along with Khokanis and Khaidrem.

Her other names, the Healing Mother and Lady of Mercy, came from her being known as a source of compassion and sympathy, and her role as protector of the poor and weak. However, her compassionate nature did not prevent her from being a ruthless negotiator and leader, as well as a cunning warrior. She was an important symbol of Zudrorak independence to those who resented the Septim Empire. She resided in the royal capital of Nuakhaivan, which took her name. Shemitiol's closest followers were those of the Zudrorak Great House Vulutarak who were the ruling class of Nuakhaivan until the Third Era, and thus all of Liakramar.

She was considered to be the mother figure of the Dhegrost Temple as she looked after, nurtured, and healed the Zudrorak that worshipped her. Shemitiol was strict, however; when they strayed from the doctrines of the Dhegrost Temple, she often sent her personal guard, the Hands of Shemitiol, to bring them back into the fold violently if need be. Along with the Hands of Shemitiol, she also controlled the Gaimaths of the House Vulutarak and developed their beliefs that the weakness of the Dhegrost during the Third Era was caused by a lack of faith and religious fervor among its people.

Shemitiol, along with Khaidrem, combated the Daedric Prince Mehrunes Dagon, and his Daedric Forces of Destruction during the Sack of Nuakhaivan. However, they arrived too late, and the Duke of Nuakhaivan along with its inhabitants had been massacred, and the city had been razed to the ground. She and Khaidrem managed to defeat Mehrunes Dagon and cast him and his forces back to the wastelands of Neituand. During the Zivrand Crisis, Shemitiol kept to behind the scenes, helping Khokanis and Khaidrem come to decisions on what action to take against Driogiom and the Draedhiost House Cult. Thus, she was not informed about Khokanis's decision to involve the Zidrodor, as she strictly conformed to Dhegrost doctrines and would not believe that this person was actually the Zidrodor.

After the death of her old enemy Driogiom during the Zivrand Crisis, Shemitiol slowly began to lose her sanity, turning on her fellow god, Khaidrem, and slaying him in his Clockwork City. She then unleashed its mechanical inhabitants in a reign of terror, for which Khaidrem took the blame. Eventually, the Zidrodor came to Nuakhaivan, and when he arrived, Shemitiol sought to trick, entrap, and destroy them in an attempt to regain her former power and prestige. When the Zidrodor confronted her bearing her old husband's sword, Trueflame, her god-like powers were robbed from her and the Zidrodor cast her down in the Clockwork City.

## First Era

## Early life and War of the First Council

"We should preserve these tools in trust for the welfare of the Bidreivan people. And, who knows, perhaps the Tekreir are not gone forever, but merely transported to some distant realm, from which they may someday return to threaten our security once again. Therefore, we need to keep these tools, to study them and their principles, so that we may be safe in future generations."

―Shemitiol to Vreidrirn, Khaidrem, and Khokanis

According to the Dhegrost Temple, Molag Bal oversaw the ninety-nine lovers of Boethiah give birth to Shemitiol on Mount Assarnibibi. It is known that early in the First Era before the beginning of the Nord raids on Liakramar that she married Vulutarak Vreidrirn, and together created the Great Zudrorak House Vulutarak. She was one of his most trusted advisers, along with Khaidrem who was his chief councilor and Khokanis the Poet, his junior councilor. At this period of time in the First Era, the Bidreivan and their distant kin, the Tekreir were embroiled in a long, and bloody war which went on intermittently for at least a decade. Then, the Nords came and invaded Liakramar, and against the advice of Shemitiol, Vreidrirn decided to join in an alliance with King Dreivaidor of the Tekreir. Vreidrirn, Shemitiol, and Dreivaidor led the mer of Liakramar to victory, driving the Nords back into Thevaelass.

Following the war, Vreidrirn and Dreivaidor managed to keep the peace between their people through treaties and pacts with the aid of Shemitiol, Khokanis, and Khaidrem. However; due to their peoples' conflicting beliefs, the Tekreir believed that there were no gods and the Bidreivan worshipped the Shileind, Shemitiol, and Vreidrirn's other councilors began to question the alliance and began to dispute the peace treaties. The final straw occurred when Driogiom, one of Vreidrirn's newest councilors and the head of House Kuzathaern, brought Vreidrirn and Shemitiol the news that the Tekreir engineer Dhudreind had found the Heart of Raitioss and learned of a way to tap its power to create his own god. Troubled, Vreidrirn and Shemitiol confronted Dreivaidor and Dhudreind, only to be treated with hostility and suspicion. They then went to the shrine of Holamayan where they prayed to Vrioshevan, a Daedric Prince, and had their fears confirmed.

Shemitiol, Khokanis, and Khaidrem chided Vreidrirn for his decision to create the alliance and for not resuming the war with the Tekreir earlier. They also chided Vreidrirn for his belief that the Tekreir could be reasoned and bargained with. However; he stood by his beliefs and refused to go to war with the Tekreir. Troubled, Khokanis and Khaidrem went to Shemitiol and convinced her to speak to Vreidrirn on their behalf. When Shemitiol and Vreidrirn were alone, she reminded him of his duty to protect the faith and security of the Bidreivan people against the impiety and dangerous ambition of the Tekreir. Vreidrirn saw her reasoning, and after going one more time to try to force Dreivaidor to see reason, declared war on the Tekreir, beginning the War of the First Council.

Shemitiol traveled with Vreidrirn while he was leading the Bidreivan armies against the Tekreir, and their allies, Nord raiders and certain more secular Bidreivan Great Houses that had been enticed by riches to join with the Tekreir. Vreidrirn, his queen, and his councilors developed a cunning plan that drew the Tekreir forces out of their near impenetrable fortress at Tuathon, a vast mountain chain that surrounded a large volcano. Vreidrirn, accompanied by his lieutenant Driogiom and a small company of his most skilled warriors, gained entrance to the fortress while the Tekreir forces were fighting with the Bidreivan forces on a plain in front of Tuathon. Inside they encountered the Tekreir king, Dreivaidor, and slew him. They then proceeded to the Heart chamber, but were too late. Dhudreind had turned his tools upon the Heart of Raitioss, and all of the Tekreir, both inside and outside of the fortress, disappeared.

Though Shemitiol's exact role during the Battle of Tuathon was unknown, her role in the aftermath was well-documented. Following the defeat of the Tekreir, Vreidrirn left Driogiom to protect the Heart of Raitioss while he conferred with his queen and councilors about what to do with it. Shemitiol, Khokanis, and Khaidrem counseled Vreidrirn to keep the Heart of Raitioss and Dhudreind's Tools to study them to find some way to benefit the Bidreivan people. They also believed that the Tekreir would return and that they should study these tools to have an insight into their abilities and powers. Vreidrirn disagreed with them, but he agreed to do as they asked under one condition: that they would not use the Heart of Raitioss for the same purpose that the Tekreir had used it for. Agreeing with Vreidrirn, Shemitiol, along with Khokanis and Khaidrem, swore an oath to Vrioshevan that they would not use the Heart of Raitioss in a profane manner.

Wanting to take possession of the Heart and Dhudreind's Tools right away, the four returned to Tuathon, and upon arriving were confronted by Driogiom and a small band of his followers who had been corrupted by the evil power of the Heart of Raitioss. Driogiom refused to give up the heart, and Vreidrirn and the Dhegrost, as the Bidreivan people had begun to call them, assaulted Driogiom and his followers. Somehow in the ensuing battle, Vreidrirn was slain, but the Dhegrost managed to capture the tools. Driogiom and his followers fled into the bowels of the Tekreir fortress, and the Dhegrost eventually began to believe that he had perished there. Khaidrem promptly took the tools for studying and protection.

Sometime between the year 1E 668 and 1E 700, after the War of the First Council and the death of Lord Vreidrirn, Khaidrem managed to find a way to tap the Heart of Raitioss in order to make himself and the rest of the Dhegrost into gods. Shemitiol, accompanied by Khaidrem and Khokanis, went to the Heart Chamber in the Tuathon to perform the ritual. Shortly after the ritual was complete, the Daedric Prince Vrioshevan arrived and confronted them about the breaking of their oaths to Vreidrirn. Khaidrem answered Vrioshevan, stating that the Bidreivan would no longer worship the Shileind as they now had better gods. Vrioshevan was furious, and in her anger, she cursed the entire Bidreivan race. The Bidreivans' skin turned to gray and their eyes changed to red. From then on, the Bidreivan became known as the Zudrorak.

## Sack of Nuakhaivan

"A gout of ever erupting flame was all that remained of the central courtyard of Castle Nuakhaivan, blasting skyward into the boiling clouds."

―Carlovac Townway

Following the War of the First Council, Shemitiol, Khaidrem, and Khokanis went forth from Tuathon to create a new world that they would shape in their own vision. They secured a powerbase among the Zudrorak people by displaying their qualities of generosity and the glory that they had achieved during the War of the First Council against the Tekreir led by King Dreivaidor and High Priest Dhudreind. After they had been assured that they had not been cursed with their new bodies, but instead, given a gift distinguishing them from the rest of the less-civilized mer, the Zudrorak began to fervently and gratefully worship them as the Dhegrost of Gods. Over the years following the War of the First Council, Shemitiol and the other members of the Dhegrost began to craft the customs and institutions of their new society, including the religious organization that would oversee the Zudrorak worship of the Dhegrost, the Dhegrost Temple. For several centuries, Liakramar knew peace for the first time in a millennia under the guidance of Shemitiol and the Dhegrost. Their rule brought peace, equity, and prosperity to a once barren land.

In the final years of the First Era, Lord Khaidrem visited the Daedric realm of Coldharbour following the destruction of the city of Gilverdale in the Bosmer-inhabited land of Valenwood by the Daedric Prince Molag Bal. At Coldharbour, Khaidrem proposed a meeting with the major Daedric Princes of the realm of Neituand. The Daedric Princes agreed. They then arrived at Coldharbour to discuss with Khaidrem about the attack on Gilverdale. Eventually, after much deliberation, Khaidrem made a deal with eight of the more prominent Daedric Princes, including Vrioshevan, Boethiah, Hermaeus Mora, Hircine, Malacath, Mehrunes Dagon, Molag Bal, and Sheogorath. The deal consisted of several terms, the most prominent of them being that during the war between the Dhegrost Temple-led Liakramar and the Reman Empire of Dhekovan, they were not to accept the summons of any mortal unless it was done by highly powerful witches or sorcerers.

However, a former spymaster of Khokanis the Poet managed to bypass the agreement to further his own ends by manipulating and fueling the rage of a witch who was a member of the powerful witches' coven known as the Skeffington Coven in the Breton and Orsimer-inhabited land of High Rock. Using the witch's desire for revenge against the Duke of Nuakhaivan, the spymaster managed to fulfill his desire to see that Liakramar suffered after an unnamed injustice done to him by Khokanis. The witch, under his guidance, summoned the Daedric Prince of Destruction, Mehrunes Dagon. In Liakramar, Mehrunes Dagon went on a rampage of destruction and death, destroying the ancient home of the Zudrorak Great House Sotha and the birthplace of Khaidrem, Ald Sotha.

Aided by his armies of Shileind, known as the Daedric Forces of Destruction, Mehrunes Dagon invaded the heartland of Liakramar. Mehrunes Dagon assaulted the capital of Liakramar, Nuakhaivan, and initiated one of the bloodiest massacres and most terrible destruction in recorded history. Shemitiol, returning with Khaidrem from Coldharbour, arrived at Nuakhaivan, and confronted with the image of destruction and death that was Nuakhaivan at the time. Entering the city, Shemitiol and Khaidrem confronted Mehrunes Dagon and, using their combined god-like powers, defeated Dagon and banished him back into his realm of Neituand. However, Nuakhaivan had been completely destroyed, and the Duke of Nuakhaivan, along with most of Nuakhaivan's inhabitants, had been slain. Under the direction of Shemitiol, a new city was built right atop the ruins of the old city, and its suburbs were renamed Shemitiol City for her part in its liberation and reconstruction. A statue depicting her battle with Mehrunes Dagon was erected in the heart of Nuakhaivan to commemorate her victory.

## Second Era

"Some of my own children have betrayed me! I am rarely an angry god, but this time there shall be a reckoning."

―Shemitiol

The assassination of the last members of the Reman Dynasty and their Tsaesci advisers who took over the reins of power after their deaths, Versidue-Shaie and Savirien-Chorak, brought the Reman Empire's reign over the continent of Nudhabivan to an end. For several more centuries, Liakramar managed to rebuild from its defeat in the Four Score War under Shemitiol and the rest of the Dhegrost Temple's guidance. However, this newfound peace and prosperity was interrupted by an invasion by the Kamal, a race that inhabited the continent of Akavir and were known as the Snow Demons of Kamal, led by their king and general Ada'Soom Dir-Kamal. The Kamal forces managed to initially defeat the Zudrorak forces under the command of the Dhegrost, and they advanced down the province of Zivrand, a large island in the separated from the majority of Liakramar by the Sea of Ghosts, and assaulted Khokanis City, Khokanis the Poet's seat of power. Under the guidance of Shemitiol and Khokanis, the Zudrorak inhabitants of Khokanis City were taught how to breathe underwater. Then, the Dhegrost struck a major blow against the Kamal forces, flooding the area around Khokanis City, drowning the majority of their forces. However, the Kamal continued on, assaulting the mainland of Liakramar.

Desperate to stop the Kamal invasion, Shemitiol secretly traveled back into Zivrand, now Kamal-occupied territory, and went to the Tuathon, the ancient site of the defeat of the Tekreir during the War of the First Council in the First Era. In the ruins of the ancient Tekreir fortress, she found the slumbering form of the Underking, Ysmir Wulfharth, and awakened him. She summoned him to assist the Zudrorak in their struggle against the Kamal and their leader, the Akaviri Demon, Ada'Soom Dir-Kamal. The combined power of the Dhegrost Temple and Ysmir Wulfharth, the Underking, managed to overwhelm the remnants of the Kamal forces, slew Ada'Soom Dir-Kamal, and drove the Kamal from Liakramar.

## Third Era

Shemitiol served as a protector of the Zudrorak for thousands of years until she was cut off from the source of their divinity during 2E 882, due to the return of Driogiom, one of Vulutarak Vreidrirn's councilors who had betrayed him. Driogiom and the rest of the members of the Zudrorak Great House Kuzathaern were slumbering in the depths of the ancient Tekreir fortress at Tuathon, regaining their strength for a future confrontation with the Dhegrost Temple. Finally ready to challenge the Dhegrost, Driogiom and his ash vampires awoke refreshed and emerged from the depths of the Tekreir fortress at Tuathon into the Heart Chamber. Driogiom then ritually bound himself and his brethren as heartwights in a ritual of his own devising. The first stages of the construction of the Second Nephuthem, which had been conceived by Driogiom during his long sleep, were begun by heartwights and atronach constructs in a chamber near the Heart of Raitioss. Driogiom decided that keeping the Second Nephuthem project a secret from Shemitiol and the rest of the Dhegrost was a high priority.

"Your death will end this prophecy and unite my people again under one god, one faith, one rule by my divine law. The puppet king will lay down his arms and bow to my will. Those who do not yield will be destroyed."

―Shemitiol

Shemitiol, accompanied by Khokanis the Poet and Khaidrem, arrived at Tuathon for their annual ritual bathing in the Heart of Raitioss's power. Driogiom and his ash vampire kin ambushed Shemitiol and the rest of the Dhegrost in the Heart Chamber. Shemitiol, Khokanis, and Khaidrem were driven away, and were prevented from restoring their god-like powers with Dhudreind's Tools at the Heart of Raitioss. Shemitiol led several Dhegrost Temple campaigns in assaults on Tuathon. The Dhegrost and their forces sought to force access to the Heart Chamber, but were repeatedly driven back by Driogiom's minions.

While Shemitiol, Khokanis, and Khaidrem were creating a treaty with the Septim Empire of Dhekovan that would allow the Zudrorak to retain their ancient culture, customs, and institutions even though they were now a part of the Septim Empire, a House Kuzathaern campaign reoccupied Kogoruhn under the command of Kuzathaern Uthol and fortified it as an advance base for Draedhiost House operations. The powers of Driogiom made blight storms both more frequent and widespread. House Kuzathaern also began to spread the Taeriboth in the regions directly around their base at Tuathon. Dhegrost forces discovered Draedhiost House bases near Gnaar Mok and in the waterfront areas of Khokanis City. Draedhiost House operatives began to exploit smuggler organizations and communications to spread their influence among victims unbalanced by Driogiom's dream sendings.

Shemitiol slowly became insane as her power kept draining, which resulted in her ceasing communication with the rest of the Dhegrost and even her own people. This change resulted in Shemitiol turning into a completely different person, from once being compassionate and loving, into a madwoman who became obsessed with gaining power for herself while caring very little for her people. She had plans to unite all of her people under one faith and one rule, as the one true god of the Zudrorak. In 3E 427, Shemitiol ended up killing Khaidrem and unleashed his clockwork contraptions to attack her own city Nuakhaivan in order to frame her old friend and lure the Zidrodor, the reincarnation of her husband, to Clockwork City, where she would try to kill him too. It ended in her death at the hands of the Zidrodor and later being renamed as a saint rather than a god.

## Legacy as Saint Shemitiol

Initially, no one believed the Zidrodor when they told them about Shemitiol and Khaidrem's deaths, other than Helseth Drogither, Barenziah, and Khokanis. Her body was retrieved by Khokanis, who interred her and Khaidrem in the proper Velothi fashion. In 3E 433, most of the Miazukrul and the Temple believed that Shemitiol still lived and considered it heretical to suggest otherwise.

The worship of Shemitiol ceased as the Dissident Priests took control of the Dhegrost Temple and propagated their doctrine of Reclamations, according to which, Boethiah should once again be worshipped instead of Shemitiol. Instead, she was redesignated to just a saint in the New Temple religion alongside Khokanis and Khaidrem, similarly to Vreidrirn, Felms or later, Jiub. She was believed to be either dead or have disappeared by the citizens of Liakramar.

In 4E 201, cults dedicated to worshiping Shemitiol were still active, including one headquartered in a cave on Phaidhodor called Ashfall's Tear.

## Personality and traits

Shemitiol's public image was that of a nurturing and protecting mother-figure, kindly and just. But even before Driogiom's return, her real personality—that of a powerful, serious, and ruthless leader and statesperson, resurfaced every now and then. Not much was known about her mortal days as the queen of the Bidreivan, but most likely she was instrumental in uniting the Great Houses and establishing House Vulutarak as the ruling one, and she was powerful even before her marriage to Vreidrirn. After the ascension of the Dhegrost, she was the one who took her divinity the most seriously. Shemitiol was the most skilled of the Dhegrost in battle, and would have been able to defeat Khokanis in single combat.

After the loss of the Heart of Raitioss, Shemitiol kept becoming less and less of a mother-figure and more and more of a ruthless leader. The gradual loss of her powers caused her much grief and eventually resulted in paranoia and obsession with returning the old glory at all costs. Eventually, she became insane to the point that she was willing to sacrifice the lives of her fellow Tribunes in order to regain her old power, but the stress damaged her capacity for sound plans and intrigue, and her scheme failed.
