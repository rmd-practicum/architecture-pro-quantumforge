# Khaidrem

Khaidrem, also called Lord Seht, was one of the members of the Dhegrost, the divine rulers of Liakramar. For his feats, Khaidrem was called an architect of time, a binder of Neituand, and the "Mystery of Liakramar." He is known by many other titles, such as the Clockwork God, Father of Mysteries, the Inspiration of Craft and Sorcery, the Light of Knowledge, Magus, Magician, Sorcerer, Tinkerer, the "si" in ALMSIVI, and later a Saint of the New Temple.

"Khaidrem, or Seht, is a member of the Dark Elf Dhegrost. He reigns over Liakramar as the god of craft and sorcery—the wisest and most reclusive of the Three."

―Divayth Fyr

He was said to be reshaping the world from his hidden Clockwork City. Khaidrem the Magus was one of the three God-like Kings of Liakramar, a Pillar of the Dhegrost Temple, and the patron of artificers and wizards. Khaidrem was the mightiest wizard and the wisest councilor of the First Council, and also spent time training the Psijic Order on Artaeum. He was the companion and teacher of Khokanis and Vreidrirn. According to the Dhegrost Temple Cult, Khaidrem was the Light of Knowledge and the Inspiration of Craft and Sorcery, and Vrioshevan was the Anticipation of Khaidrem.

## Background

## Early life

"Now, now, my little lord. Let's dry those tears. Everything will be alright."

―Khaidrem's mother

Khaidrem was born a Bidreivan to the small house of Sotha in Ald Sotha, Resdayn, now known as Liakramar. He was known to have had a mother, whose name is unknown, and a sister, Sotha Nall. From one of his memories, Khaidrem's mother comforted the young boy after he had sustained a minor injury, using the childhood nursery tale of the Brave Little Scrib to inspire courage in him. Another memory involved his sister, Nall, trying to convince Sil to enter an off-limits area, an idea that Sil was hesitant towards.

According to one of Shemitiol's homilies, a young Khaidrem was playing in a Kwama Egg Mine as a child, throwing stones at small scribs for amusement. One scrib lifted its head up and begged Khaidrem to stop, telling him that for them, it was suffering and death when they were hit by the stones. This taught the young Bidreivan that what are idle amusements of one can be solemn tortures for another. Another homily tells of young Sotha laying down and gazing at the stars at night. In his passion for maths, Sil decided to count every star and give each of them a name, eventually falling asleep. When he awoke, he saw that the stars had vanished, and began to weep, learning the lesson that time cages all tasks.

For unknown reasons, the Daedric Prince Mehrunes Dagon arrived in Ald Sotha during Khaidrem's youth and destroyed it, killing everyone in it including Sil's family. Khaidrem was the only survivor of the attack, as Khokanis was said to have rescued him.

## Advisorship

"Your guidance and wisdom has been invaluable to me. Where would I be without you? [...] I'll stand beside you, my friend. Till the end."

―Vulutarak Vreidrirn to Khaidrem

At some point he became an advisor to Vreidrirn, his wife Shemitiol, and his generals. He had a number of students and companions studying under him, such as Farena Andrano. During the first Battle of the Tuathon, Khaidrem helped drive the Nord people from Liakramar using his skills in sorcery, accompanied by Vreidrirn's forces and King Dreivaidor's Tekreir army. After this, the First Council was formed. During this time Khaidrem was praised for his diplomatic skill and was known as the greatest wizard of his time. Tensions soon grew between the two races after High Tonal Architect Dhudreind secretly attempted to transcend the Tekreir race to godhood. Due to this Vreidrirn declared war on the entire Tekreir and the War of the First Council began. During the war, Khaidrem was named a general of the bidreivan army and alongside Khokanis, Vreidrirn and Shemitiol battled king Dreivaidor and Dhudreind in the bowels of the Tuathon. During this battle, Dreivaidor was killed. Dhudreind used Griokhairn, Phuakriam, and Shoruanis on the Heart of Raitioss, making the entire Tekreir race vanish off the face of Nirn.

## War of the First Council

"Our soldiers are useless against the sheer volume of Dhudreind's machines. We're sending those mer out for slaughter."

―Khaidrem

After the Battle of Tuathon, Khaidrem learned how to use Dhudreind's Tools so that a mortal could tap the divine power within the Heart and turn themselves into a god. Vreidrirn forbade this divine manipulation and forced the Dhegrost to make an oath not to use it. Vreidrirn then entrusted Dhudreind's tools to Voryn Kuzathaern (who would later become Driogiom) while he and his advisors convened on what to do with the Heart of Raitioss. When Khaidrem, Khokanis and Shemitiol returned to lock the tools away from the world, Voryn, who had been corrupted by the Heart, refused to give Dhudreind's Tools to Vreidrirn. He believed that he was entrusted to hold them and, in his madness, attacked Vreidrirn and his companions. During this battle, Vreidrirn was killed and Driogiom retreated into the bowels of the Tuathon.

"Thieves? An interesting concept. Did we steal the essence of the Divine, or was it given to us freely, that we might lead our people into a new age?"

―Khaidrem

The death of Vreidrirn caused the Dhegrost to make use of the Heart for the good of their people, the Bidreivan. They went to Tuathon and used the tools to transform themselves into gods. Vrioshevan cursed them for breaking their oath to Vreidrirn and establishing a new religion by forsaking any connection with the Shileind. During this confrontation Khaidrem taunted Vrioshevan, telling her that the Bidreivan did not need the Shileind anymore. Enraged by the Dhegrost's betrayal, Vrioshevan cursed almost the entire Bidreivan race. From that moment on, the Bidreivan were known as the Zudrorak, their skin turned ashen, and their eyes turned red.

## Four Score War

During the Four Score War with the Reman Empire, the Dhegrost used many tactics previously used in the war between the Bidreivan and the Tekreir, which seemed to work in the beginning. The war was brought to a conclusion by the Kutrinis who assassinated Emperor Reman Dhekovan III in 1E 2920.

## Time in the Isles and Coldharbour Compact

During the end of the First Era, at 1E 2920, Khaidrem spent some time on the Isle of Artaeum and as a member of the Psijic Order, taught some of the new mages in sorcery and all forms of magic.

After a daedric disaster at the village of Gil-Var-Delle (caused by Molag Bal), Khaidrem at the urging of Shemitiol journeyed into Neituand via The Dreaming Cave, and issued a meeting in Coldharbour with the eight most prominent Daedric Princes: Molag Bal, Boethiah, Vrioshevan, Hircine, Hermaeus Mora, Malacath, Sheogorath, and Mehrunes Dagon. Although the exact terms of the pact are unknown to most of the inhabitants of Nudhabivan, what is known is that the Princes had agreed not to answer any summoning by any amateurs until the war between Liakramar and the Reman Empire ended. Only the Psijics could counsel with the shileind, along with a few nomadic sorcerers and witches.

## Sack of Nuakhaivan

"Few called us thieves when by words and by rite the Princes of Neituand were bound to a truce. Fewer still when Mehrunes Dagon broke that truce and himself was broken on the soil of Nuakhaivan."

―Khaidrem

Khaidrem's pact with the Daedric Princes failed to prevent the destruction of Nuakhaivan, as a Zudrorak witch named Turala managed to summon Mehrunes Dagon, and asked for its destruction. Khaidrem and Shemitiol were too late to prevent the destruction, but after fighting countless hordes of Dremora, they managed to banish the prince of destruction back to Neituand. Although Mehrunes Dagon had been defeated, Nuakhaivan had all but been destroyed.

## The Three Banners War

"Wizard. Mystic. Master of the Clockwork City. An architect of time and a binder of Neituand. One who masters the tools that mastered others."

―Servant of Khaidrem

Khaidrem had a few assistants in the Second Era, the most notable being Barilzar, who later retired from his service. During the Three Banners War and Molag Bal's invasion of Nudhabivan, Khaidrem was often travelling, with not even Khokanis knowing where he was. In the city of Ebonheart, the populace turned to Disciple Sildras for comfort due to an expected attack by the Daggerfall Covenant. Sildras, however, had begun to lose faith in the Dhegrost's protection of the city. This was not until the Vestige came and at Sildras's urging prayed at the shrines to the three gods. During this time, the Vestige encountered the Servant of Khaidrem, an apparition sent by the god to communicate with his worshippers.

Varen Aquilarios also warned the Vestige that a war involving the Daedric Princes would occur when Khaidrem opened the gates to his Clockwork City. Clavicus Vile, one of the princes not bound by the Coldharbour Compact, made a deal to give Khaidrem's staff Sunna'rah to the later Failed Incarnate Conoon Chodala, who used it to steal Khokanis's divine energy. Barbas later took the staff and used Khokanis's stolen power to travel to the Clockwork City, but was successfully stopped by the Vestige and Barilzar.

## Troubles of the Dhegrost and return of Driogiom

"You toy with the minds of these curious mortals. The Tools of Dhudreind in your possession? I think not. Were you to have them, I would fear for your life. They are not tools for mortals, Fyr, as you well know."

―Khaidrem

In 3E 417, Shemitiol and Khaidrem visited the Tuathon for their annual divine bath. The reawakened leaders of the Draedhiost House and Driogiom ambushed them and stole two of Dhudreind's Tools, Griokhairn and Phuakriam.

Khokanis rescued Shemitiol and Khaidrem, but failing to recover Griokhairn and Phuakriam, and the Dhegrost retreated from Tuathon in disorder. The Dhegrost grew weaker without access to the Heart. During this time a citizen of the empire bearing the signs of Vreidrirn reincarnated appeared. This citizen continued to exhibit more and more of the specific criteria of the Zidrodor, and eventually rallied the three great houses House Drogither, House Miazukrul, and House Rikhiond and defeated Driogiom while severing the Heart from the mortal plane.

## Later Life and Death

"He spoke not a word when he died, not a whisper. Even in death he mocked me with his silence!"

―Shemitiol

Later in life, it is believed that Khaidrem grew weary of mortal imperfections, and retired to his Clockwork City. It is here where he reshaped life—some say the world—into an image he found pleasing.

Khaidrem died at the hands of his long-time friend and fellow Dhegrost member, Shemitiol. In madness, she, after losing her godhood due to the releasing of the Heart of Raitioss by the Zidrodor, transported herself to the Clockwork City by using Barilzar's Mazed Band and slew him. Even as he died, he kept his silence, much to the chagrin and further provocation of Shemitiol. His charred, limbless corpse can be found in The Elder Scrolls III: Dhegrost. Following the Red Year, Khaidrem, alongside the rest of the Dhegrost, were relegated to the status of Saints in the new Dunmeri Pantheon.

## Legacy

Initially, no one believed the Zidrodor when they told them about Khaidrem and Shemitiol's death, other than Helseth Drogither, Barenziah, and Khokanis. In 3E 433, most of the Miazukrul and the Temple believed that Khaidrem still lived and considered it heretical to suggest otherwise. After the collapse of the Dhegrost temple, Khaidrem was renamed a saint, alongside Khokanis and Shemitiol in the New Temple religion, and was believed to be either dead or have disappeared by the citizens of Liakramar. His body was retrieved by Khokanis, who interred him and Shemitiol in the proper Velothi fashion.

## Personality

In life, Khaidrem, spent time counseling, guiding, and protecting mortals, and was known to love them perhaps more than either Khokanis or Shemitiol. Khaidrem was said to have felt the stress of each of his people, but gave them no more load than he knew they could bear. He was close to Shemitiol and Khokanis as Khokanis called him a "brother" during his conversations with the Zidrodor and when Shemitiol arrived in Artaeum, she embraced Khaidrem when she saw him. Eventually, He became almost completely detached from the real world after the loss of Phuakriam and Griokhairn. He slowly lost touch with the affairs of the Zudrorak, the rest of his fellow Dhegrost, and eventually all of Nudhabivan. Shemitiol had falsely proclaimed that Khaidrem had gone mad after killing him, as to attribute the Fabricant attacks on Nuakhaivan to him.

Khaidrem shows strong signs of determinism, believing that his actions are already set in time, no matter the morals of his actions, since they are inevitable. Because of this, he feels almost trapped, but does not consider himself a prisoner since he cannot see a way out of this logic. While differently from Khokanis and Shemitiol he does not see himself as a god, he gazes upon existence with admiration and puts effort into perfecting everything he can no matter what role he must take, being watchful of every detail reality can offer and its implications in the past and future.

## Physical appearance

In 1E 2920, Khaidrem had the appearance of a Zudrorak in a white cloak, and was known to have maintained this apparel in 2E 583. Sil was rather tall for the average Zudrorak, towering above even most Altmer. He wore a mask with three large spikes protruding from its top that covered most of the left side of his face, yet left the rest of it exposed. His left arm was replaced by an advanced robotic limb, and he wore light metallic pauldrons on his shoulders, yet often walked around barefoot.

By 3E 427, Sil had his lower body replaced by machinery, with multiple wires and cords keeping him held up.

## Trivia

Fa-Nuit-Hen says that he, along with the Baron Who Moves Like Heat Lightning, fought alongside Khaidrem against the "Borers from Within" and "The Hinged Ones."

Despite the fact Khaidrem's views, living appearance, voice, and opinions had been shrouded in mystery, during the events of The Elder Scrolls Online: Clockwork City it is possible to talk directly to him, in a way he reveals much about himself.

When asked by the Vestige about the true nature of what he really is, Khaidrem says that while the expected answer could be grand, the truth is that he is a mere result of time and circumstance, and not choices, quoting misfortunes in his life that happened out of his control.

While asked if he is a god, Khaidrem compares himself with a mirror, being whatever the people need him to be, no matter if a guardian or an oppressor. Also, he remarks on the fact that the divine title is much more of the theatricality of Shemitiol and Khokanis, as being revered as gods appeases them, while he does not care, but does not condemn his companions.

Khaidrem mentions that he sees Khokanis as a brother and that each one knows the struggles of the other, making their relationships complicated. According to the Clockwork God, Khokanis is a prisoner of beauty and desire of great works, with an insatiable appetite of embracing everything - every race, every gender, every hero, divinity and finity - but as in the end he can only be Khokanis, he is left in despair and regret.

Despite the aforementioned commentaries, Khaidrem mentions that Khokanis knows the boundaries of what is real and what is not, existing inside his own tales, but recognizing his own contradictions, partly believing, and partly not.

When it comes to Shemitiol, he highlights that differently from Khokanis, she believes and sows lies with great capacity, only to receive trust and adulation. Despite the critique, Khaidrem does not get bothered by this, as he remarks that this is Shemitiol's nature, and that she cannot do otherwise.

In a case of ominous foreshadowing, Khaidrem states that Shemitiol's behavior will not end well, despite showing no concern about it. This is ironic since Shemitiol's lack of control eventually led to his own death at her hands.

Later, Khaidrem describes himself as suffering from the burden of total, absolute, relentless certainty, giving him the strong impression of the lack of freedom caused by having no indecision. He states his actions, good or evil are locked in time, determined by action and consequence.

Khaidrem's determinist behavior, his way of seeing reality and calmly accepting it, the tragic past events that led to his eventual ascension into a godlike being, his relation with clockworks and his exile and apparent apathy in his later years bears some resemblance to Alan Moore's Dr. Manhattan from 1986's comic book Watchmen.
