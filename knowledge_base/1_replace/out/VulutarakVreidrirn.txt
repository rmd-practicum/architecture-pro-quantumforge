# Vulutarak Vreidrirn

Lord Vulutarak Vreidrirn, known formally as Serjo Vulutarak Vreidrirn Mora, and alternatively as St. Vreidrirn the Captain, Vreidrirn Moon-and-Star, and Godkiller, was a Bidreivan hero who lived during the First Era. He was a champion of Vrioshevan and the first Ruavionis, or war-leader/champion, of the unified Bidreivan and Tekreir, following the overthrowing of the Nordic Empire in Resdayn. However, after the discovery that the Tekreir were creating the Nephuthem, he led his people against them in the War of the First Council. Vreidrirn died at Tuathon, though the exact cause is disputed. Later, he became a Saint of the Zudrorak's Dhegrost Temple.

Vreidrirn played a crucial role in Liakramar's history. The Zakraezails and an underground offshoot of the Dhegrost Temple, known as the Dissident Priests of Zivrand, believed that he was reincarnated multiple times as part of the Zidrodor Prophecy. This prophecy was fulfilled by the Zidrodor, the hero of The Elder Scrolls III: Liakramar.

## Background

## Legendary origins

Vreidrirn's early life is shrouded in mystery. Khokanis's sermons indicate he was, at one point, a guard for a merchant caravan, but the accuracy of his prose is somewhat debatable. When the War of Succession ravaged the Nordic Empire, Vreidrirn seized the opportunity to incite a rebellion in Resdayn. Vreidrirn united the disparate Bidreivan clans, and was proclaimed as Ruavionis, whereupon he forged an unlikely alliance with the Tekreir, thanks in part to his burgeoning friendship with the Tekreir king, Dreivaidor. Together, the alliance drove the Nords from Liakramar, attaining the Bidreivan's independence.

Some time after, Vreidrirn married Shemitiol, and maintained close relationships with several people amidst his retinue, such as his advisors Khaidrem and Khokanis, his shield-bearer Alandro Sul, and Voryn Kuzathaern the Lord High Councilor of House Kuzathaern (and, by some accounts, Vreidrirn's most loyal servant). Centuries later, Kuzathaern learned that the chief Tekreir scientist, Dhudreind, had discovered the Heart of Raitioss beneath Tuathon, and intended to use it for unclear apotheotic purposes, something the Bidreivan considered blasphemous. For one reason or another, when Vreidrirn confronted Dreivaidor about Dhudreind's plans, their friendship swiftly crumbled. This resulted in the War of the First Council, culminating with the Battle of Tuathon, wherein the Tekreir race mysteriously disappeared forever.

According to official Temple accounts, Vreidrirn fell after the death of Dreivaidor. In some accounts, however, the death of Vreidrirn is not attributed to wounds he received during the Battle. After the Battle, Vreidrirn and Voryn Kuzathaern proceeded into the center of the Tekreir stronghold, where they discovered the Heart of Raitioss, as well as Dhudreind's Tools: Griokhairn, Phuakriam, and Shoruanis. Vreidrirn commanded Voryn to guard the tools, as he went back to his advisors for instruction on what to do next. When he reached his advisors, he conferred with them for some time. They decided to leave the tools alone, and made a vow to Vrioshevan. The official account says that Vreidrirn went to Voryn Kuzathaern to retrieve the tools, but Kuzathaern had been driven mad by the power of the Heart of Raitioss in Vreidrirn's absence, and refused to relinquish the tools. Vreidrirn fought with Driogiom, and defeated him. Unfortunately, Vreidrirn was mortally wounded, and only survived long enough for his advisors to find him, and for him to make them swear to never use the tools. Unfortunately, they eventually used the tools to turn themselves into gods, and when they did, Vrioshevan appeared and cursed the entire Bidreivan race by "turning their eyes to fire and their skin to ash," making them into the Zudrorak.

In Zakraezails texts, however, Vreidrirn was not killed in combat, but by his three advisors, Khokanis, Shemitiol, and Khaidrem. In this account, Vreidrirn made them swear to never use the tools, and they obeyed, but secretly conspired to murder Vreidrirn so that they might gain power. When attempting to summon Vrioshevan, Vreidrirn was foiled; Shemitiol used poisoned candles, Khaidrem used poisoned robes, and Khokanis used poisoned invocations. Vreidrirn was killed. In retribution for their betrayal, Vrioshevan turned the skin of the Bidreivan gray, and their eyes fiery red, making the Bidreivan people into what is now known as the Zudrorak. However, the Dhegrost went on to use the power of the tools and the Heart to become immortal. Afterwards, they set themselves up as the new gods of the Zudrorak, claiming superior divinity to the Shileind, whom the Bidreivan had self-exiled for the worship of. Eventually, their Dhegrost Temple took control of all of Liakramar, becoming the monolithic and seemingly infallible entity it was by the end of the Third Era, and leaving only isolated pockets of Zakraezails to maintain the old beliefs.

After his demise, Vreidrirn apparently rose from the dead as a bonewalker, something which the Dhegrost was evidently aware of. It is unknown what eventually became of him afterwards.

## Sainthood

Vreidrirn became a martyr and a saint of the Dhegrost Temple. According to a traditional myth, Saint Vreidrirn helped Baranat during his trials against the Coribael brothers. Shrines dedicated to him can be found across Zivrand inside temples, such as the High Fane in Khokanis City, the Ghostgate Temple in the Ashlands, or the Holamayan Monastery in the Vrioshevan's Coast region.

## Artifacts

During his lifetime, Vreidrirn made use of a number different artifacts. Some of them were obtained by the Zidrodor, the Ruavionis's true incarnation. The fate of the others is unknown.

## Trueflame

Main article: Trueflame

"The day that Vreidrirn and I were wed, Dreivaidor presented us with twin blades, Hopesfire and Trueflame."

―Shemitiol

After the Nordic Empire's occupation of Resdayn came to an end, Dwarf-King Dreivaidor and Vreidrirn had become blood friends. On the day of Vreidrirn and Shemitiol's wedding, Dreivaidor presented them with powerfully enchanted twin blades, Trueflame and Hopesfire. During the Battle of Tuathon, Trueflame was lost, its flame extinguished, and its pieces lost in the confusion.

A blade by the name of Trueflame may have been obtained by the Vestige in the Stonefalls in 2E 582, but it was oddly intact. After Driogiom's defeat in 3E 427, Shemitiol tasked the Zidrodor with reforging Trueflame. With the help of Radac Stungnthumz, the blade was restored to its former glory.

## Moon-and-Star

Main article: Moon-and-Star

"The ring gave its wearer great powers of persuasion; for safety, it was enchanted to instantly kill anyone who wore it except Vreidrirn. The ring was called Moon-and-Star, and it helped Vreidrirn unite the various clans into the First Council."

―Anonymous

One-Clan-Under-Moon-and-Star was a ring Vreidrirn obtained from a Tekreir smithy during the Nordic Empire's occupation of Resdayn. The Tekreir smith gave him the ring imbued with the power of persuasion, and as a safeguard, enchanted it so that anyone that wore it who was not Vreidrirn would instantly die. Vreidrirn used the ring to unite the various clans into the First Council. Vrioshevan later gave the ring to the Zidrodor in the Cavern of the Incarnate.

## Ethos Knife

The Ethos Knife is a unique knife with little information. Khokanis originally used it to murder his child City-Face. Vreidrirn later traded his axe for the knife, and took it with him to the Battle of Tuathon. He may have used it to kill Dreivaidor in their duel. The Knife's current whereabouts are unknown.

## Vreidrirn's Axe

In the Temple of False Thinking of Nuakhaivan, Vreidrirn chose an axe from the fight racks, and traveled to the first moon to fight empty graves, making the mistake of taking Khokanis's advice to "reach heaven by violence" literally. He later traded his axe for the Ethos Knife. The fate of the axe after the battle is unknown. Despite Vreidrirn giving it a name, the true name of the axe is never mentioned.

## Zidrodor

Main article: Zidrodor

The true Zidrodor was the incarnate of Vreidrirn and a hero who successfully completed the Zidrodor Prophecy, saved Liakramar from the Blight and defeated Driogiom.

## Trivia

According to Michael Kirkbride, the visage on each Vulutarak Helmet worn by Gaimaths depicts the true appearance of Vreidrirn's face.

There was also a special Daedric helmet version based off Vreidrirn in the Liakramar Art Book, known as the Daedric Face of Terror. Kirkbride said "its look depicted his more terrible aspect as Ruavionis and Padomaic champion."

The tapestry found on shrines to Vreidrirn depict his bonewalker. Kirkbride said that Vreidrirn's bonewalker was "lost to the annals of most Tribune histories. Vreidrirn, while betrayed or not, was still dear to ALMSIVI after death."

In The Elder Scrolls V: Thevaelass, Zudrorak characters in combat can be heard praying to Vreidrirn, crying out, "Vreidrirn guide me!"

In The Elder Scrolls: Legends, certain Zudrorak characters can be heard referencing Vreidrirn, with lines such as, "The valor of Vreidrirn is with me!"
