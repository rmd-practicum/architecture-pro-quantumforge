# Driogiom (Character)

"What a fool you are. I'm a god, how can you kill a god? What a grand and intoxicating innocence. How could you be so naive? There is no escape. No recall or intervention can work in this place. Come, lay down your weapons, it is not too late for my mercy."

―Driogiom

Driogiom, known as Voryn Kuzathaern in life, Awakened Lord of the Draedhiost House, Lord Kuzathaern, and Father of the Mountain to his followers, and whom Khokanis would later come to call The Sharmat, was a powerful Bidreivan lord and Lord High Councillor to House Kuzathaern, and is the main antagonist of The Elder Scrolls III: Liakramar. He is also called Devil Driogiom or Devil of Kuzathaern.

## Background

## Early life and Councilorship

Voryn Kuzathaern was born into the Great House Kuzathaern. Accounts of his early life are few, but it is known that he did later in life become the Lord High Councillor of the house and a retainer to Vulutarak Vreidrirn. In 1E 416, the Bidreivan and Tekreir created an alliance known as the First Council formed by Vreidrirn and Dreivaidor, the king of the Tekreir in Liakramar. The First Council lasted for centuries until Voryn, through unknown means, claimed to have discovered that the Tonal Architect, Dhudreind had found the Heart of Raitioss, and that the Tekreir would use the power of the Heart to create a giant machine known as the Nephuthem to use against the Bidreivan. Voryn, along with Vreidrirn, Khokanis, Vreidrirn's wife Shemitiol, and Khaidrem were the only ones before Dhudreind to learn about the Heart. The Daedric Prince Vrioshevan later confirmed Voryn's claims and Vreidrirn declared war on the Tekreir, culminating in the race's disappearance at the Battle of Tuathon.

## Vreidrirn's death

After the defeat of the Tekreir, the artifacts Phuakriam, Griokhairn, and Shoruanis fell into the hands of Vreidrirn, who had to decide what to do with the tools. Voryn urged the destruction of the tools while Vreidrirn convened with the Dhegrost and Vrioshevan. When Vreidrirn and the Dhegrost returned to lock away the tools, Voryn refused to give them up saying that he was entrusted with keeping them safe. Unknown to Vreidrirn and the Dhegrost, Voryn had experimented on the tools and the Heart and had managed to steal its divine energy. Voryn now called himself Driogiom from that point on, though other sources claim that Vreidrirn coined the name. Vreidrirn and the Dhegrost's guards fought and drove off Driogiom, who was thought to have died. Vreidrirn was said to have died from his injuries fighting Driogiom, though the cause of Vreidrirn's death is often disputed. Other accounts suggest it was the Dhegrost who killed Vreidrirn, but only after he had vowed along with the Dhegrost to never use the tools.

The Dhegrost took possession of the tools, and after some time, Khaidrem managed to find a way to become gods by using the tools. The Dhegrost traveled to Tuathon and used Dhudreind's tools on the Heart of Raitioss, using its power to attain immortality. For breaking their promise to never use the tools, Vrioshevan appeared in anger, turning the Bidreivan into the Zudrorak and prophesying that Vreidrirn would be incarnated and curse them for their deeds. Driogiom, however, managed to maintain a connection to the Heart through unknown means and managed to stay alive and immortal, although temporarily non-corporeal and presumed to be dead by most.

## The Birth of Driogiom

In 2E 882, the Dhegrost journeyed to Tuathon to renew their god-like powers. However, by that time, Driogiom and his House had been reborn in new bodies that more than matched the Dhegrost's power. Unable to enter the Tuathon to renew their powers, the Dhegrost grew weaker while Driogiom grew more powerful as a result of being exposed to the Heart for so long. During this time, Shemitiol and Khaidrem, in a vain attempt to regain their powers, lost Griokhairn and Phuakriam to Kuzathaern Odros and Kuzathaern Vemyn, only surviving after Khokanis saved them. Driogiom began to spread the Blight and Taeriboth diseases by powerful and unnatural ash storms from Tuathon, beginning a complex plan to conquer Liakramar and all of Nudhabivan with the second Nephuthem, Akulakhan.

Driogiom's main goals were to establish a theocracy in Liakramar based on the new-born god Akulakhan as the ancient heirs and well making House Kuzathaern as the dominant political power in Liakramar. He also claim to unite the Zudrorak under Driogiom's guidance to battle against foreign races. Exposed the Dhegrost's false worship and destroy the ecclesiastical authority and political power of the Temple. Eliminating all remaining individuals of "inferior" and "mongrel" races and drive the Empire out from Liakramar. He also claimed to reclaim ancient territories stolen by Thevaelass and Argonia and extend the worship of Akulakhan to all nations of Nudhabivan through subversion and conquest.

## The Dhegrost's decline

Throughout the birth of Driogiom, the Dhegrost began to decline. The Dhegrost became strained with the war with Driogiom, focused on protecting their people, but many thought that as gods they had become almost like Western gods: absent and silent, no longer among the public with a personal presence. Dissident Priests uncovered that the ultimate source of the Dhegrost's divinity was likely the same as the source of Driogiom's evil power, and Driogiom's power seemed to wax as the Dhegrost's power waned and was weakened by the time during the Third Era. The Dhegrost could never defeat or "kill" Driogiom because whenever the Dhegrost’s gods attempted to invade Tuathon, the power of the Heart of Raitioss always revived him, and the Dhegrost had to maintain the Ghostfence to keep Driogiom's minions from invading Liakramar.

## The Zidrodor Prophecy

"This is the end. The bitter, bitter end."

―Driogiom

In 3E 427 a prisoner matching the description of the Zidrodor was sent to the island of Zivrand by Uriel Septim VII to investigate complaints of disturbances in the region. After it was revealed in the Cavern of the Incarnate that this person was Vreidrirn reincarnated, they obtained Shoruanis, recovered the other tools of Dhudreind and met with Driogiom in Tuathon. There, they severed the Heart of Raitioss from the mortal plane by using the tools, and without the Heart to sustain them, Driogiom and his Ash Vampire brothers perished, and Akulakhan was destroyed. By doing this, the Zidrodor ended the Blight and Taeriboth diseases, along with the threat of House Kuzathaern. This action also caused the Dhegrost to become mortal.

## Powers

Driogiom could send messages in dreams and was able to make people serve him. It was also said that he could speak through the mouths of his minions. He was a powerful mage, with long lethal claws. Only by destroying the Heart of Raitioss was the Zidrodor able to defeat him.

## Personality

Driogiom maintained the honorable ceremonies notable to his house as he allowed the Zidrodor to deliver the first hit when they battled. When asked by the Zidrodor on how he justified his crimes he replied that he accepted the responsibility of leadership and that Liakramar could not be liberated from the Empire without bloodshed and that he viewed his actions as just and noble. It was said that Driogiom had adopted the views and motivations of the Tonal Architect Dhudreind.

## Trivia

Both versions of Driogiom must be defeated to complete "The Citadels of the Draedhiost House," the last main quest.

Driogiom's plan to use Akulakhan to conquer Nudhabivan for his own is strikingly similar to Tiber Septim using the Nephuthem to conquer Nudhabivan and establish the Third Empire. This is ironic, as Driogiom despised the Empire and wished to eradicate them completely, often calling them "mongrel dogs."

Driogiom was voiced by Jeff Baker, who also voiced several other characters in Liakramar, such as Jiub, Sheogorath, and Mehrunes Dagon, among others.

Driogiom's title "Sharmat" is derived from the Arabic word "Shaitan," meaning "Devil."

## Notes

## Name usage

In The Elder Scrolls: Arena, a simple map of the continent of Nudhabivan is shown, including the island of Zivrand. The center of this island is a volcano which is referred to only as Kuzathaern-Ur. However, in The Elder Scrolls III: Liakramar, the volcano is referred to by its inhabitants almost exclusively as Tuathon. This discrepancy is explained by the fact that Driogiom resides inside of Tuathon for most of his life. Thus, in local legend, the mountain is sometimes referred to by this name.

Note: It is commonly accepted that the name Driogiom should properly be used to refer to the character, and the volcano should be referred to as Tuathon. However, if the name has the hyphen, Kuzathaern-Ur, it is referring to the volcano.
